# 5\. URL Discovery Protocol

For every seed URL:

1. Open the page and record the URL actually reached.
2. Record the final URL after redirects.
3. Record the page title and visible page type.
4. Follow relevant first-party MCA links.
5. Discover child pages, service cards, forms, FAQs, documentation and related services.
6. Identify authentication boundaries and transaction entry points.
7. Record legacy, duplicate, redirected, dead-end and inaccessible routes.
8. Do not treat a plausible-looking URL as valid without verifying it.
9. Do not use third-party websites as evidence for MCA functionality unless the third party is explicitly part of an MCA transaction.
10. Where a current page and older MCA documentation conflict, record the discrepancy rather than silently resolving it.

# 7\. Evidence & Assumption Rules

| **Classification**  | **Use**                                                                                          |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| VERIFIED            | Directly supported by a current official MCA page, official document or observed live behaviour. |
| INFERRED            | Reasonable conclusion from public evidence but not explicitly documented.                        |
| PROPOSED            | A future-state recommendation. Do not mix it with current-state facts.                           |
| UNKNOWN             | Insufficient public evidence to determine the answer.                                            |
| VALIDATION REQUIRED | Important item that should be confirmed with MCA/process/system owners.                          |

Never convert INFERRED or UNKNOWN information into a factual statement merely because it appears technically plausible.

# 15\. Backend / System Reconstruction Rules

The objective is to identify what the public evidence implies about the system, not to reverse-engineer imaginary internals.

- Identify publicly documented MCA21 V3 modules and capabilities.
- Separate visible frontend behaviour from backend process assumptions.
- Identify master-data dependencies where prefill or entity lookup is visible.
- Identify transaction identifiers such as SRN where applicable.
- Identify payment dependencies and observable payment states.
- Identify document repository/access patterns.
- Identify automated versus human processing where officially documented.
- Identify officer/central-processing workflows only to the extent publicly evidenced.
- Mark databases, queues, microservice boundaries, event buses and internal APIs as UNKNOWN unless publicly documented.

# 17\. What Must NOT Happen in Phase 1

- Do not redesign the UI.
- Do not start production frontend development.
- Do not start production backend development.
- Do not assume the existing MCA information architecture is the correct future architecture.
- Do not treat old V2 documentation as proof that a route is currently live.
- Do not invent internal SOPs.
- Do not invent databases or service boundaries.
- Do not claim an integration is confirmed unless evidence supports it.
- Do not collapse person, account, role, professional capacity, entity and authorisation into one concept.
- Do not create page-specific logic where the current-state evidence suggests a reusable service/process concept.
- Do not proceed automatically into Phase 2.

# 19\. Quality Gate Before Phase 2

Phase 1 is complete only when Kiro can answer the following:

- What are the actual major entry points into MCA today?
- What are the major service families?
- What services exist beneath each family?
- Which services are transactional versus informational?
- Which forms/webforms support each major service?
- Who uses each service?
- What entity does each service operate on?
- What are the major prerequisites and dependencies?
- What data is entered versus prefilled?
- What documents and authentication are required?
- Where does payment occur?
- What happens after submission?
- Which processing paths appear automated versus human?
- What states can a transaction enter?
- What happens when something fails or requires resubmission?
- What registry/document/case outcome is produced?
- What is the next user obligation?
- Which legal sources can be traced to each service?
- What is known about the e-governance modules?
- Which parts remain unknown and require MCA stakeholder validation?

# 20\. Phase 1 Stop Condition

**STOP AFTER PHASE 1.**

Do not proceed to future-state product architecture, technical architecture, design-system implementation or large-scale frontend development until the current-state findings have been reviewed.

The review should specifically challenge incorrect assumptions, missing services, obsolete URLs, incomplete workflows, wrong role mappings and unsupported backend conclusions.
